After dinner, Mom took a stack of gold paper out of the cabinet, and Lili and Xiaoming gathered around curiously.  
“Kids, in just a few days it will be the Zhongyuan Festival. We’re going to fold gold joss paper ingots,” Mom said with a smile.  
Mom sat down and slowly taught them how to fold the gold paper into little boat-like shapes—those were the ingots.  

While folding, Lili looked at the gold paper in her hand and asked, “Mom, I don’t quite understand. How can the ancestors receive this money?”  
Xiaoming immediately added, “I think I know! When the ancestors come back at night, we burn it for them, right?”  

Dad happened to come by and heard what they said. He smiled. “Xiaoming is right. Every year in the seventh month, the ancestors’ spirits come back home. Come, let Dad tell you a story.”  
The two children put down the paper and ingots and sat beside him.  
Dad said that many years ago, their great-grandfather was a very kind person. He always helped the neighbors harvest wheat. One year, there was a heavy rain in the village, and he even went to help repair houses. Later, when great-grandfather passed away, everyone missed him deeply.  

After listening to the story, Lili said, “I think great-grandfather must be very happy that we still remember him.”  
Xiaoming nodded and said, “I think so too. Then let’s fold some extra ingots to send to him!”  

Mom nodded, and the three of them continued folding ingots together. First they folded in half, then pressed gently—the little basin soon filled up with gold ingots. Once the ingots were folded, the sky had slowly grown dark.  

Mom brought out washed apples and pastries. Dad placed a small table in the middle of the courtyard and neatly arranged the food on it. Then he took out three incense sticks and carefully lit them. The incense smoke rose slowly, drifting up into the night sky.  
Dad said softly, “Come, let’s bow together and welcome the ancestors home.”  
Lili and Xiaoming copied Dad and earnestly bowed three times. The evening breeze blew gently through the yard; it was very quiet, with only the fragrance of incense. They felt that the ancestors must have seen them and must be very happy.  

The candlelight on the table flickered softly. The family stood in the courtyard, feeling warmth in their hearts. They sat quietly for a while, and then Mom shared the fruit with everyone. They ate together. Lili thought to herself, it’s truly wonderful to remember the ancestors together with family.

## Grammatical Points and Analysis

### Verb-Object Phrases:

- **折元宝 (zhé yuánbǎo)** – fold joss paper ingots  
  This is a common verb-object phrase where the action (“fold”) directly takes a target object (“ingots”). It appears multiple times in the story, e.g., “我们要折金元宝了。”

- **讲故事 (jiǎng gùshi)** – tell a story  
  Another verb-object construction. In the story, the father says, “爸爸给你们讲个故事。” The verb “讲” (tell) and the object “故事” (story) combine naturally.

### Resultative Complements:

- **听完 (tīng wán)** – finish listening  
  “完” after the verb “听” indicates that the action is completed. In the line “听完故事，丽丽说…” it means after the story was fully heard. The result is that listening reached its end.

- **折好 (zhé hǎo)** – finish folding (properly)  
  “好” as a resultative complement indicates completion with a satisfactory result. In “折好了元宝，天也慢慢黑了,” it shows that the folding was finished and the ingots were ready.

- **摆好 (bǎi hǎo)** – set up / arrange properly  
  Similarly, “好” indicates that the action of arranging (“摆”) achieved the desired state. The text says “把食物整齐地摆好,” meaning the food was placed neatly and ready.
