## English Translation

On the evening of Qixi Festival, the weather was very pleasant. Lili and Xiaoming set up a small table in the courtyard, with fruits and Lili’s handmade needlework on it. The stars were bright, and the moon was beautiful too.

Lili said to Xiaoming, “Let me tell you the story of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl.” Xiaoming nodded happily.

Lili said, “Long ago, the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl fell in love. But the Queen Mother of Heaven used the Milky Way to separate them. Every year on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month, magpies build a bridge so they can meet.”

After listening, Xiaoming looked at the sky and asked, “Lili, can we see the magpie bridge?”

Lili smiled and said, “We can’t see a real one, but let’s make paper magpies to decorate the courtyard and make a wish.”

They found colored paper and scissors and made paper magpies. Lili taught Xiaoming how to fold them. They folded many red, blue, and yellow ones, and hung them on the tree branches with thread.

When the wind blew, the paper magpies swayed as if flying. Lili made a wish: “I wish for a happy family and good needlework skills.” Xiaoming imitated her and made a wish too: “I wish for happiness and progress in my studies.”

They sat eating fruit, watching the paper magpies and the stars. Lili said, “Today is really beautiful.” Xiaoming said, “Yes, very happy.”

The night deepened, they tidied up and went back inside. Xiaoming placed a favorite paper magpie by his pillow. They will remember this Qixi Festival.

## Grammatical Points and Analysis

### Verb-Object Phrases
Many common actions in the story follow the verb-object pattern. Pay attention to how these fixed combinations work:
- 放桌子 (fàng zhuōzi) – to set a table (lit. “put table”)
- 讲故事 (jiǎng gùshi) – to tell a story
- 搭桥 (dā qiáo) – to build a bridge
- 做手工 (zuò shǒugōng) – to do handiwork / needlework
- 许愿 (xǔyuàn) – to make a wish
- 吃水果 (chī shuǐguǒ) – to eat fruit
- 装饰院子 (zhuāngshì yuànzi) – to decorate the courtyard

### Resultative Complements
A resultative complement follows a verb to indicate the outcome of the action. The structure is **Verb + Result**.
- 听完 (tīng wán) – to finish listening: 听 (listen) + 完 (finish)  
  *小明听完故事* – Xiaoming finished listening to the story.
- 看见 (kànjiàn) – to see (successfully): 看 (look) + 见 (perceive)  
  *我们能看见喜鹊桥吗？* – Can we see the magpie bridge?
- 收拾好 (shōushi hǎo) – to tidy up completely: 收拾 (tidy) + 好 (done well)  
  *他们收拾好回屋里* – They tidied up and went back inside.
- 挂在 (guà zài) – to hang (and the result is the location): 挂 (hang) + 在 (at)  
  *用线挂在树枝上* – hung them on the branches with thread.

### 把 (bǎ) Structure
The 把 sentence moves the object before the verb to emphasize what happens to it. The pattern is **Subject + 把 + Object + Verb + Result/Detail**.
- *小明把一只纸喜鹊放枕边。* – Xiaoming placed a paper magpie by the pillow.  
  (The paper magpie is “dealt with” by being placed.)
- *用线把纸喜鹊挂在树枝上。* – (They) used thread to hang the paper magpies on the branches.  
  (The paper magpies are the ones being hung.)

### 地 (de) for Adverbial Modification
When an adjective describes how an action is done, 地 is placed after the adjective.
- *高兴地点点头* – nodded happily (高兴地 = happily)
